The best time for a mock interview is a few days before the real one, while there's still time to fix what it finds. The AI interviewer reads your résumé and the job description, asks one question at a time, and pushes back when an answer is thin. You get a scored report a minute after you finish.

Book an interview

  1. Open it
    In the menu, go to Mock interviews → AI mock interviews and tap New interview.
  2. Describe the interview
    Type the role, pick the industry and the kind of interview, then choose a difficulty and a length. Each length shows roughly how many questions you'll get.
  3. Add your résumé and the job
    Pick a saved résumé, upload one, or paste the text. For the job description, choose From a lead if the job is already in Jobtiva, Paste it if it isn't, or General preparation when you don't have a specific job yet.
  4. Pick your topics
    Choose up to 10. The interviewer makes sure each one comes up.
  5. Book it
    If your plan includes an interview, it's booked straight away. Otherwise the button shows the price, and you pay online to book.

The prices in this picture are only an example; the form always shows the current ones. A job description from a lead gives the sharpest questions, because the interviewer can quiz you on that exact job. If the lead has no description saved yet, open the job's link, copy it and paste it into the lead first.

Every kind of interview here is a spoken, one-on-one conversation, like a phone screen or a round with a hiring manager. There's no code editor or whiteboard, and nothing to upload during the interview, so it's best for rounds where how you explain yourself matters most.

Before you press Start

Your booking waits on the AI mock interviews page until you start it, up to the date under Valid until (30 days after booking). Until then you can change the role, topics, résumé and job description with Edit setup. The difficulty and length stay as booked, since they set the price.

Tapping Start opens a short Get ready page. Give yourself the full length plus up to 10 minutes: if time runs out mid-answer, the interviewer takes a few minutes to wrap up. Once you press Start interview, the booking is used and can't be started again from the beginning.

During the interview

  • Answer the question in the box at the bottom and tap Send. On a computer, Enter sends and Shift+Enter starts a new line.
  • To speak instead, tap the mic. It works in Chrome, Edge and Safari. Your words appear in the box, so read them before you send. Say "comma", "full stop", "question mark" or "new paragraph" for punctuation. "Period" doesn't count, because people say "notice period" in answers.
  • A short or vague answer gets a follow-up. That's the interviewer doing its job, not a sign you failed.
  • You won't get hints or scores along the way. All the feedback is in the report.

The timer only counts while you're connected. If your Wi-Fi drops or you close the tab, come back within 15 minutes and you'll carry on from the same question with the time you had left. To stop early, tap End. Only the questions you answered are scored.

Read your report

It's usually ready in under a minute, and we email you when it is. You'll find it later under History, or with View report on the interview's row.

The top shows your score out of 100, how it compares with your last interview, your strongest skill and the one to focus on. Under Your skills, five skills are scored out of 10: Communication, Technical knowledge, Confidence and clarity, Problem-solving and Answer relevance. A small line on each bar marks your last interview, so you can see which way each one moved.

Then come What went well and What to improve (tap Read the details for the full notes), and Answers to work on, which lists the answers that missed something, what was missing, and a better answer for each. Read those better answers out loud. It's the quickest way to make them your own.

Your action plan at the end is what to practice before the next round. When you've worked on it, tap Practice again. A score only tells you something next to the one before it, and Performance shows how each skill moves across your interviews.
